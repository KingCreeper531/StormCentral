***From Previous Discussion***

A highly amplified upper level pattern has developed over the 
eastern Pacific and it appears southwest California will be 
experiencing quite a range of temperatures and weather conditions
over the next couple of weeks. The upper low over the area
yesterday and today that has brought scattered light showers and 
much cooler temperatures will evolve into our first real Santa Ana
wind event of the season later in the week. For today though,
highs will mostly be in the 60s which is 10-20 degrees below with
some off and on light showers, mainly in the morning.

As the upper low slowly moves into the Great Basin, onshore
pressure gradients will be weakening and a light northeast flow
aloft will develop over the area as early as Tuesday afternoon.
Not expecting any winds of significance Tuesday but high
temperatures will warm up 4-8 degrees area-wide as the air mass
dries out and heights rise at least 10dam from Monday.

Wednesday will be noticeably warmer and drier as offshore 
gradients continue to increase across the area heights climb 
another 10dam. Still not expecting any significant winds yet
except possibly some northeast winds in the San Gabriel mountains
up to 35 mph.

.LONG TERM (THU-SUN)...12/339 AM.

Santa Ana winds will really pick up Thursday into Friday as all
the ingredients appear be in place and properly aligned. We're 
still well outside the boundaries of the high res models but given
the amplified upper level pattern, confidence is higher than 
usual at this early stage that a Santa Ana Wind event of at least 
moderate strength will develop during this period. The latest 
ensemble pressure gradients indicate that the peak offshore 
gradient will occur Friday morning somewhere in the 5-7mb range. 
Couple that with 50kt winds at 850 mb and very strong upper level 
subsidence, that should result in peak winds of 50-70mph in the 
San Gabriel mountains and 35-55mph across the LA/Ventura valleys 
as well as the Ventura County coastal areas and Santa Monica 
mountains. The ECMWF is already showing peak winds between 25 and 
40 mph in Camarillo Thursday and increasing to 35-50 on Friday. As
a reminder, Santa Ana winds typically do not impact the LA Metro 
area and LA coast. 

Temperatures will be peaking during these days as well with highs
in the upper 80s and 90s across all coast and valleys, including
the Central Coast. There is a very strong chance (60-80%) that
heat advisories will be needed for coastal areas Fri and Sat. 
However, mid 90s in the inland areas would not likely meet 
criteria there.

Temperatures are expected to remain well above normal through
Sunday but slowly cooling into next week. And looking out a little
further there is fairly high agreement in the models that another
cold upper low will move into the area the middle of next week.
